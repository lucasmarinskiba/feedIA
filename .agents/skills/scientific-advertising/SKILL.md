# Claude C. Hopkins — Scientific Advertising (1923)

**Core**: Advertising = science not art. Test everything. Measure results. Eliminate waste. Build brand through performance metrics.

**Era**: 1920s direct mail/print. But framework is timeless (works for digital, social, email today).

**Guiding Principle**: "People don't want ads. They want the product. Ads are bridge."

## Core Principles

### 1. TEST EVERYTHING

- Split test headlines, copy, offers, design
- Small tests before big spend
- "Don't gamble with large ads. Test with small ones first."
- Coupon codes = proof of response
- Track which ads produce customers (not just impressions)

### 2. SPECIFICITY > VAGUE CLAIMS

- ❌ "Best product"
- ✅ "Reduces shipping weight by 40%, saves $200/year"
- ❌ "Trusted by millions"
- ✅ "Over 2.3M units sold in Q3 2023"
- Specific numbers = believable. Vague = ignored.

### 3. SAMPLING & COUPONING

- Let them try risk-free
- Free sample > best salesman pitch
- Coupon = built-in tracking device
- "Money-back guarantee" = removes objection
- Creates quantifiable result

### 4. POSITION FOR YOUR MARKET

- What do customers NEED (not what you want to sell)
- "Brake linings" → Position as "Safety device"
- "Soap" → Position as "Medical cleanliness"
- Frame around their desire, not your product

### 5. REASON-WHY COPY

- Every claim needs reason/proof
- ❌ "Our coffee is best"
- ✅ "We roast beans within 2 hours of shipment. Stale coffee = oxidized oils. Fresh = flavor preserved."
- Reason why = credibility bump

### 6. ELIMINATE COMPETITION THROUGH SPECIALIZATION

- Don't compete on price (race to bottom)
- Own a position (niche, feature, benefit)
- "Only product with X" > "Cheaper than competitor"
- Build unfair advantage through specificity

### 7. FOCUS ON CUSTOMER BENEFITS, NOT FEATURES

- ❌ Feature: "Triple-insulated walls"
- ✅ Benefit: "Stays hot for 12 hours. Save $3/day on coffee runs."
- Customer buys result, not specs

## Copywriting Structure

1. **Headline** — Attention. Benefit-driven or curiosity.
2. **Reason Why** — Why this works. Proof/science behind it.
3. **Social Proof** — How many use it. Testimonials. Results.
4. **Call to Action** — Coupon, code, next step. Make it easy.
5. **Risk Reversal** — Money-back guarantee. Remove objection.

## Testing & Metrics

- **Control group** — Current best ad (baseline)
- **Test group** — New headline, copy, offer
- **Measurement** — Actual customer acquisition per $$ spent (not impressions)
- **ROI mindset** — Every ad spend = investment requiring return

## Examples from Book

**Pepsodent Case**:

- Before: Toothpaste generic, low demand
- Hopkins: Positioned as "prevent tooth decay," offered samples
- Result: Created mass market for toothpaste (single biggest category creation of era)

**Palmolive Case**:

- Reason why: "Contains palm and olive oils for gentleness"
- Benefit: Hands stay soft while washing dishes
- Result: Dominant brand for decades

## Anti-Patterns

- ❌ Big vague claims ("Best ever!")
- ❌ No reason why (claim without proof)
- ❌ Feature-focused (spec sheets = boring)
- ❌ No risk reversal (high objection)
- ❌ No test data (guessing)
- ❌ One-size-fits-all message (should vary by audience)

## Modern Application

1920s principles = 2020s digital:

- Headlines (email subject lines, ad copy)
- Reason why (testimonials, case studies, proof)
- Sampling (free trial, lead magnet)
- Couponing (promo codes, special offers)
- Positioning (niche market)
- Testing (A/B tests, variant testing)
