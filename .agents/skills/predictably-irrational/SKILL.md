# Dan Ariely — Predictably Irrational (2008)

**Core**: Humans aren't rational. Irrationality is systematic, predictable, exploitable. Why turquoise jewelry sells when price doubles.

## 13 Predictable Irrationalities

**1. Relativity** — Everything relative, never absolute. $100 watch seems expensive. Same watch next to $1,000 watch seems cheap. Anchor high first.

**2. Supply & Demand Fallacy** — Price not based on value. Diamonds valuable because rare. Price artificially kept high = creates perceived value. Scarcity (real or fake) drives demand.

**3. Zero-Cost Effect** — FREE is powerful psychological trigger. "FREE shipping" > "Ship for $0.01". Costs us nothing but feels like gain. Loss aversion activated.

**4. Social Norms vs Market Norms** — Two worlds collide:

- Social: "Help me move" (free, relationship-based)
- Market: "I'll pay you $20 to help me move" (now transactional, relationship dies)
- Mixing norms breaks things. Don't charge for favors among friends (kills generosity).

**5. Arousal Effect** — Emotional state (hot/cold) changes decisions. Hot (aroused, in love) = irrational. Cold (rational) = clear thinking. Hormones override logic. Hot decisions = regrets later.

**6. Procrastination & Self-Control** — We're terrible at delaying gratification. Knowing we will procrastinate ≠ stops us. Need external structure (deadlines, commitments, stakes) to force action.

**7. Ownership Bias** — We overvalue what we own. Own a mug = value it higher than non-owners would pay. Sunk cost fallacy. "I have it" = I overvalue it.

**8. Keeping Doors Open** — Options distract from main goal. Too many options = analysis paralysis. Closed doors (no option) = focus. Paradox: more options = less satisfaction (Barry Schwartz).

**9. Expectations** — Mind gets what it expects. Expensive wine tastes better (brain literally processes it differently). Expectations shape perception. Marketing shapes expectations = shapes experience.

**10. Power of Price** — 50-cent aspirin works better than penny aspirin. Price = signal of efficacy. Expensive = must work better. Placebo effect real. Pricing is psychology.

**11-12. Dishonesty & Cash** — Context of character matters. We cheat incrementally (rationalize small thefts). Cash makes us honest (abstract vs concrete). Handling money = visceral reminder of value.

**13. Behavioral Economics Framework** — No free lunches. Every decision has trade-offs. Irrationality systematic = predictable = profitable for those who understand it.

## Activation for Agents

```
"Apply Ariely irrationality principles:
RELATIVITY: All decisions relative to anchor. First anchor wins. High anchor > everything else seems cheap.
ZERO-COST: Free is psychological trigger (not rational). Worth more than penny. Loss aversion activated.
SOCIAL VS MARKET: Never price favors among friends (kills relationship). Separate worlds collide dangerously.
AROUSAL: Emotional state overrides logic. Hot (aroused) = irrational. Cold (calm) = clear. Account for emotional state in pricing/messaging.
OWNERSHIP BIAS: Customers overvalue what they already own. Sunk cost. Use in retention (they've invested).
EXPECTATIONS: Price shapes perception. Expensive = works better (even if identical). Expectations literal.
PROCRASTINATION: Willpower fails. External structure (deadline, stakes) required. Internal motivation insufficient.
ANCHORING + PRICE: Price first, high. Everything downstream = relative to anchor. Psychology, not logic.
Anti-patterns: Rational-only messaging, low anchor first, ignoring expectations/context."
```
