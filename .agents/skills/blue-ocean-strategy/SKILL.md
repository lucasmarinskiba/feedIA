# W. Chan Kim & Renée Mauborgne — Blue Ocean Strategy (2005/2017)

**Core**: Stop competing in red oceans (bloody price wars). Create blue oceans (uncontested markets).

**Guiding Principle**: Competition is irrelevant. Create new market space instead of fighting existing one.

## Red Ocean vs Blue Ocean

### RED OCEAN (Competitive)

- Compete on existing industry rules
- Try to beat competitors
- Capture existing demand
- Race to bottom (price/features)
- Value-cost tradeoff (pick one)
- Result: Commoditized, low margins

### BLUE OCEAN (Market-Creating)

- Create NEW rules. No competitors.
- Make competition irrelevant
- Create new demand
- Differentiation at low cost
- BOTH high value + low cost
- Result: Premium pricing, growth, uncontested space

## The Four Actions Framework

### 1. ELIMINATE

What should be eliminated that industry takes for granted?

- Example: Cirque du Soleil eliminated animal acts (costly, controversial)
- Question: What can we stop doing?

### 2. REDUCE

What should be reduced below industry standard?

- Example: Cirque eliminated star performers (high cost)
- Reduced spectacle to lower production cost
- Question: What's overkill in this industry?

### 3. RAISE

What should be raised above industry standard?

- Example: Cirque raised artistic sophistication
- Theater-quality narrative, character, acrobatics mastery
- Question: What do customers really value that's being ignored?

### 4. CREATE

What should be created that industry never offered?

- Example: Cirque created "artistic circus" (circus wasn't about art before)
- Created NEW value proposition (not just "cheaper" or "more features")
- Question: What unmet need could we fill?

## Value Innovation

**Blue Ocean = Value Innovation** (not just innovation)

- Focus on value customers receive (not just R&D for R&D's sake)
- Simultaneous pursuit of differentiation + low cost
- ❌ Red Ocean: "Quality OR price"
- ✅ Blue Ocean: "Quality AND low cost"

## Strategy Canvas (Visualization Tool)

Plot competing factors (Y-axis) vs competitors (X-axis):

1. **What to eliminate** — Factors industry obsesses over but customers don't value
2. **What to reduce** — Below industry standard
3. **What to raise** — Above industry standard
4. **What to create** — New factors no one offers

Visualize the gap (your new line) vs competitor lines.

## Examples

### Airline Industry (Southwest)

- **Red Ocean**: United, American, Delta compete on routes, service, lounges
- **Blue Ocean**: Southwest eliminated hub-and-spoke, frequent flyer lounges
- **Raised**: Fast turnaround, friendly service, fun brand
- **Created**: Low-cost + fun (not "cheap AND miserable")

### Automotive (Tesla)

- **Red Ocean**: Ford, GM, Toyota compete on MPG, horsepower, features
- **Blue Ocean**: Tesla eliminated gas engines, dealerships
- **Raised**: Acceleration, technology, environmental
- **Created**: Electric luxury (wasn't a category before)

### Healthcare (Minute Clinic)

- **Red Ocean**: Hospitals, urgent care compete on specialists, comprehensive care
- **Blue Ocean**: Eliminated specialists, complex diagnostics
- **Raised**: Speed, convenience, price transparency
- **Created**: "Quick fix" clinic for non-emergency (wasn't a market before)

## Focus on Non-Customers

- Who COULD be customers but aren't?
- What's stopping them from using existing offerings?
- In healthcare: Non-customers = people avoiding doctors (price/time/complexity)
- Blue ocean = remove barriers for non-customers

## Strategic Pricing

- Not "lowest cost" (race to bottom)
- Not "premium" (only for few)
- **Smart pricing**: 80-90% of leader's price, 100% more features
- Attracts mass market (switchers) without undercutting

## Anti-Patterns

- ❌ "Benchmark competitors" (keeps you in red ocean)
- ❌ "Beat them on their factors" (race to bottom)
- ❌ Differentiation alone (increases cost)
- ❌ Only targeting existing customers
- ❌ Ignoring non-customers (where real growth is)
