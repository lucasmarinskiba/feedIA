# Jim Collins — Good to Great: Why Some Companies Make the Leap... and Others Don't (2001)

**Core**: Good = enemy of great. Most good companies plateau. Rare few make leap → 15+ year sustaining. Research of 11 companies vs comparison group revealed systematic patterns.

## 9 Chapters (Key Findings)

### 1. Good is the Enemy of Great

- Complacency trap. Good companies complacent = plateau.
- Excellence exceptional (not default). Majority never become great because satisfied with good.
- Question: Can good company become great and sustain it?
- **Answer**: Yes. Study found 11 companies that did.

### 2. Level 5 Leadership

- Paradox: Greatest CEOs blended humble + fierce determination.
- Level 5 Hierarchy: Individual Contributor → Competent Manager → Effective Manager → Visionary Leader → **Level 5** (Humble + Ambitious).
- Level 5 = personal humility + professional will. Attribute success to others/circumstances, take blame personally.
- Companies without Level 5 stayed good. Level 5 were gateways to great.

### 3. First Who... Then What

- Traditional: Define strategy → hire people fit it.
- Great companies: Get right people on bus, wrong off. Then figure out where to drive.
- Right people = more important than strategy/vision.
- **Key insight**: People = not primary asset to manage (unlike traditional thinking). Management task = build great team.

### 4. Confront the Brutal Facts (Yet Never Lose Faith)

- Paradox: Maintain unwavering faith you'll win (long-term) + confront harsh current reality (short-term).
- Not denial. Not cheerleading. Face facts + chart course despite facts.
- Culture that allows dissent + debate without fear of punishment.
- Leaders who hear bad news early survive; denial leaders crash.

### 5. The Hedgehog Concept (Simplicity within Three Circles)

- "Hedgehog" = simple, clear core idea. Fox = chases many ideas, gets nowhere.
- 3 Circles: (1) What you deeply understand + can be best at, (2) What drives economic engine, (3) What you're passionate about.
- Intersection = Hedgehog. Not vague mission. Specific, focused concept.
- Great companies identify hedgehog + organize entire enterprise around it.

### 6. A Culture of Discipline

- Not tyranny. Not anarchy. Disciplined people + disciplined thought + disciplined action.
- People who don't need to be managed (self-discipline).
- Focus = discipline. Say no to good ideas to focus on great.
- Freedom within framework (clear core, freedom within it).

### 7. Technology Accelerators

- Myth: Great companies use technology first. Reality: Technology amplifies what exists.
- Technology not cause of greatness. Tool for acceleration.
- Good → Great companies adopted technology aligned with Hedgehog, not trendy tech.
- Wrong application of right technology ≠ leads to greatness.

### 8. The Flywheel and the Doom Loop

- **Flywheel**: Consistent push → moment → breakthrough → more push → acceleration (exponential).
- No single moment of breakthrough. Sequence of turns. Momentum builds.
- **Doom Loop**: Alternative pattern. Reactive. Chaotic. No consistent thrust. Exhaustion.
- Great companies = flywheels. Good companies = doom loops.

### 9. From Good to Great to Built to Last (Sustainability)

- Transition ≠ enough. Must sustain 15+ years.
- Maintaining discipline + culture + Hedgehog = key to sustainability.
- Management succession planned (not crisis replacement).
- Endurance > quick wins.

## 11 Good-to-Great Companies (Research Results)

Walgreens, Kimberly-Clark, Nucor, Southwest, Abbott Labs, Fannie Mae, Gillette, Bristol Myers, Wells Fargo, Philip Morris, Kroger.

Average cumulative stock return 6.9x market (vs comparison group 1.3x) over 15-year sustaining period.
