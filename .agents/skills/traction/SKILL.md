# Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares — Traction (2014)

**Core**: Traction = quantitative evidence of customer demand. Growth is what defines startup. Most fail on distribution, not product.

**Guiding Principle**: "Traction trumps everything." Technical, market, team risks easier to address WITH traction.

## 19 Traction Channels

### Content & Awareness

1. **Viral Marketing** — User referrals. Word-of-mouth loops.
2. **Public Relations (PR)** — Traditional media (newspapers, TV, magazines)
3. **Unconventional PR** — Publicity stunts, exceptional customer service
4. **Search Engine Optimization (SEO)** — Organic search visibility
5. **Content Marketing** — Blogs, tutorials, guides driving leads
6. **Speaking Engagements** — Talks, conferences building authority
7. **Community Building** — Passionate users creating network effects

### Paid Channels

8. **Search Engine Marketing (SEM)** — Google Ads, paid search
9. **Social & Display Ads** — Facebook, Twitter, YouTube, Reddit ads
10. **Offline Ads** — TV, radio, billboards, magazines (underused by startups)

### Engineering & Product-Led

11. **Engineering as Marketing** — Free tools, widgets driving leads (HubSpot Grader model)
12. **Targeting Blogs** — Niche blog outreach, sponsorships
13. **Existing Platforms** — App Store, Facebook, marketplaces with huge audiences

### Business & Sales

14. **Business Development (BD)** — Strategic partnerships (AOL + Kayak case)
15. **Sales** — Direct sales processes, consultative
16. **Affiliate Programs** — Commission-based partner referrals (GoDaddy model)

### Events & Relationships

17. **Trade Shows** — Industry-specific conferences
18. **Offline Events** — Sponsorships, meetups, conferences
19. **Email Marketing** — Retention, monetization, conversion

## Bullseye Framework (Finding Your Channel)

**Problem**: Too many channels. Which one to focus on?

**Solution**: Bullseye = 3 rings

- **Outer**: Brainstorm all 19 channels (consider all, not just "obvious")
- **Middle**: Rank by effort/reach/confidence
- **Inner**: Prioritize top 3-5 to test heavily

**5-Step Process**:

1. **Brainstorm** — List all 19. Which could work?
2. **Rank** — By effort (low/high), reach (small/large), confidence (low/high)
3. **Prioritize** — Pick 3 with high reach + low effort OR high confidence
4. **Test** — Run 2-week sprints on each. Measure results
5. **Focus** — Double down on winner. Ignore others.

**Then repeat**: As you grow, secondary channels often emerge.

## Traction Thinking

**Key insights**:

- Most founders only test channels they know (bias)
- Hard to predict winner. Test, don't guess.
- One channel often dominates (Peter Thiel insight)
- Secondary channels activate AFTER primary works
- Product + traction development = parallel (not sequential)

## Critical Path (Parallel Development)

Focus on single traction goal. Ignore everything not required.

- Example: "Get 1,000 users in 30 days via SEM"
- What's minimum product needed? Build that.
- What's minimum testing needed? Do that.
- Ignore: fancy UI, features not needed to hit goal, perfect product

## Channels by Stage

- **Early**: Warm outreach, business development, PR, speaking
- **Growth**: SEO, content, paid ads, affiliate programs
- **Scale**: Existing platforms, viral loops, partnerships

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Testing all channels at once (spreads too thin)
- ❌ Dismissing channels as "not for us" without testing
- ❌ Expecting channel to work overnight (most take 6-12 weeks)
- ❌ Not tracking metrics properly
- ❌ Giving up on winner too early
