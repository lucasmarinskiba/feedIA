# Rob Fitzpatrick — The Mom Test (2013)

**Core**: Don't ask "Is my business a good idea?" People lie, even (especially) moms. Ask questions that surface truth about their behavior/problems, not opinions about your idea.

**The Mom Test Rule**: A set of questions so good that even your mom (who loves you) can't lie to you about them.

## Why Customers Lie

- Want to be polite (don't hurt your feelings)
- Don't want to admit they don't care
- Can't imagine the product yet (hypothetical sucks)
- Feel awkward saying "no" directly
- Confuse "interesting idea" with "I will buy this"

**Result**: False positives. You feel validated but build what nobody wants.

## Good Questions vs Bad Questions

### ❌ Bad (Invite Lying)

- "Do you think this is a good idea?" → Everyone says yes
- "Would you use this?" → Hypothetical lie
- "Is this a problem for you?" → Vague. "Yeah, kinda" doesn't count
- "How much would you pay?" → Nobody knows till they actually buy

### ✅ Good (Surface Truth)

- "What's the hardest part about [task]?" → Specific problem. Forces real answer
- "How are you solving this now?" → Reveals actual behavior (gold)
- "Tell me the last time you did [behavior]?" → Anchors to specific incident (not hypothetical)
- "What would have to be true for you to do [X]?" → Uncovers hidden constraints
- Listen to stories, not opinions. "I tried X and Y but neither worked" > "Yeah I have that problem"

## How to Have Good Customer Conversations

### 1. ASK SPECIFIC, SITUATIONAL QUESTIONS

- Not: "Do you struggle with email?"
- Yes: "When was the last time email wasted your time? Walk me through it."
- Get concrete examples. Stories > opinions.

### 2. LISTEN FOR SPECIFICITY

- "Sort of" / "I guess" / "Yeah, kinda" = BS
- "I tried X, then Y, then gave up" = Real behavior
- When they're vague, ask "Can you give me a specific example?"

### 3. ACKNOWLEDGE YOU DON'T UNDERSTAND

- "I may be misunderstanding something..."
- Ask permission to dig: "Is that okay to explore?"
- Don't defend your idea. Listen.

### 4. TAKE NOTES (BUT STAY PRESENT)

- Write real quotes, not summaries
- Sketches, not judgment notes
- Maintain eye contact + conversation (don't disappear into notebook)

### 5. KEEP IT CASUAL (NOT FORMAL MEETINGS)

- Coffee shop > conference room
- Conversation > interview
- 20-30 min > hour-long
- Ask people busy with actual work (they're proxies for market)

## Red Flags in Conversations

- They say yes to everything → You're not asking real questions
- No specific examples given → No signal
- Compliments without commitment → Nice lies
- "I'd definitely use this" but won't meet again → Not serious
- They keep talking about the problem but avoid your solution → Solution isn't right

## Commitment & Advancement

Real interest shown by:

- **Explicit**:Agree to buy/use, refer someone, take next meeting
- **Monetary**: Pay for something, prepay for access
- **Effort**: Introduce you, take time to set up
- No commitment = no signal. "That's interesting" alone = worthless

## Finding Right Customers to Talk To

- **Avoid**: Friends/family (natural liars), your followers (biased), people with time to kill
- **Target**: People actively doing the behavior, struggling with it now, spending money on related solutions
- **Screeners**: "Have you paid money for X in last year?" = high signal

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Long-winded elevator pitch before listening
- ❌ Asking for feedback on your solution instead of understanding their problem
- ❌ Confirmation bias (only hearing "yes")
- ❌ Bad metrics (promises to use >> actual usage)
- ❌ Avoiding hard questions ("Does anyone actually use this?")

## Activation for Agents

```
"Apply Mom Test principles:
AVOID LYING QUESTIONS: Never ask 'Is this a good idea?' Ask for specific stories of actual behavior.
SITUATIONAL & SPECIFIC: Get concrete examples, not opinions. 'When did X happen?' > 'Do you struggle with X?'
LISTEN FOR BEHAVIORS: Past behavior > hypothetical future. 'I tried X and Y' > 'I would use this'
RED FLAGS: Yes to everything, no examples, compliments without commitment = bad signal.
COMMITMENT SIGNALS: Explicit (meet again, buy), monetary (prepay), effort (introduce, setup).
AVOID: Friends, followers, polite people. Target: Active practitioners, current pain, spending money.
Anti-patterns: Pitch before listen, ask feedback not problems, confirmation bias, weak metrics."
```
