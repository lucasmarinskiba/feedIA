# Character — Robert McKee (2021)

**Author**: Robert McKee  
**Core Concept**: "Characters are metaphors for humanity. A character is finished art; a person is work-in-progress."

## Framework Central

**Character ≠ Person**: People experience more than express. Characters express everything they experience. Fictional character = MORE layered, complex, memorable than real humans (more time to study them).

**Character arc**: REVEAL inner nature through conflict (rarely CHANGE core self). Pressure exposes true identity.

**Plot = Character + Causality**: Plot-driven (events cause character choices) vs Character-driven (character choices cause events). False dichotomy — both are same coin. Event = what happens to character; character = who causes/reacts to event.

## 10 Faculties That Shape Writers

1. **Taste**: Disgust with banality, eye for vital vs. lifeless
2. **Knowledge**: Observe self, research humanity, read voraciously, travel, experience life
3. **Originality**: Unearth hidden truths, combine forgotten influences into new wholes
4. **Showmanship**: Entertain + reveal truth simultaneously
5. **Awareness of Reader/Audience**: Craft character moment-by-moment impact
6. **Mastery of Form**: Story form comes first (character invented to serve story needs)
7. **Hatred of Clichés**: Know history, spot recycled ideas, innovate
8. **Moral Imagination**: Understand binaries (life/death, love/hate, justice/injustice) deeply
9. **An Ideal Self**: Channel best possible self when writing (concentration, honesty, sensitivity at maximum)
10. **Self-Knowledge**: All character creation begins/ends in understanding own humanity

## Characterization vs True Character

**Characterization** = outer traits (age, gender, dress, speech, job, personality, mannerism).
**True Character** = inner nature (morality, mentality, emotionality, willpower, deepest values).

Characterization = mask. True character = revealed only when life pressures character to choose.

**Principle of Choice**: Character expresses true nature through RISK-FILLED choices under high-stakes conflict. No stakes = choice meaningless. Maximum stakes = choice most revealing.

## Dimensions = Contradiction

**Flat character** = one consistent trait/worldview.
**Dimensional character** = opposing traits creating contradiction (kindness + cruelty, wisdom + foolishness, courage + cowardice).

Dimension = lived contradiction. Character shifts between poles under pressure, revealing layers.

**Complex character** = 2+ dimensions in dialogue, creating psychological depth (Walter White: teacher vs kingpin).

## Character Creation Methods

### OUTSIDE-IN (Invention)

Start with behavior/trait → imagine psychological root. Example: Suzanne Collins fused reality-TV (game shows) + Iraq War footage → Katniss Everdeen.

### INSIDE-OUT (Subconscious Birth)

Character emerges mysteriously in scenes/dialogue snippets, coalesces over time (Elizabeth Strout, Anne Lamott approach).

**Reality**: Most writers toggle both. Subconscious delivers surprises; conscious craft shapes them.

## 4 Researches That Feed Creativity

1. **Personal Research** (Memory): Tap own emotional experiences — pain, anger, love, regret. Character uses similar emotions, transformed into different context.

2. **Imaginative Research**: Walk character through 360° in mind. Don't list traits; observe them.

3. **Book-Bound Research**: Read psychology, sociology, history relevant to character. Validate + surprise own knowledge.

4. **Grounded Research**: Locate character's world in reality. Witness actual settings, people, professions. Become authority through deep immersion.

## Vision of Human Nature — Nature vs Nurture

**Intrinsic (Nature)**:

- Socrates: "Know thyself" — self-knowledge precedes wisdom
- Buddhism: Self is illusion
- Taoism: Live in harmony with inner spirit

**Extrinsic (Nurture)**:

- Aristotle: Well-lived life = public virtue, societal fulfillment
- Hinduism: Reality is illusion, self is real
- Confucianism: Hierarchy + social order define well-being

**Character design requires personal theory** of what makes people do what they do.

## Common Character Archetypes

- **Protagonist**: Drives action, pursues desire, makes choices under pressure
- **Antagonist**: Opposes protagonist (villain, rival, force of nature, internal conflict)
- **Mentor**: Guides protagonist (NOT Action-genre; appears in Maturation/Education subgenres)
- **Ally**: Supports protagonist (emotional, practical, comedic support)
- **Trickster**: Chaos agent (reveals truths through deception, disruption)
- **Guardian**: Threshold keeper (tests protagonist worthiness)

## Anti-Patterns

- ❌ Cardboard roles (no dimension, no contradiction)
- ❌ Character doesn't earn arc (undergoing change without risk)
- ❌ Psychological cliché (misunderstood genius, tortured artist)
- ❌ Melodramatic motivation (tragedy = excuse for dysfunction)
- ❌ Lack of self-knowledge from writer (project fantasy not truth)
- ❌ No research (character sounds generic, lacks specificity)
- ❌ Character is author's mouthpiece (didactic preaching, loss of character autonomy)

## Prompt Activación (Character Design)

```
"Apply Character framework:
- Characterization vs True Character: Outer traits are mask. Reveal inner nature through risk-filled choice.
- Dimensions: What contradictions live in this character? (kindness + cruelty, fear + courage)
- Principle of Choice: Higher stakes = truer character revelation. What does character choose when everything at risk?
- Research: Personal (own emotions transformed), imaginative (observe 360°), book-bound (psychology/history), grounded (witness real setting)
- Archetype: Protagonist/Antagonist/Mentor/Ally/Trickster/Guardian? (Know template so you can subvert it)
- Vision: Nature vs Nurture? Intrinsic (self) or Extrinsic (society) shaped this character?
- Test: Can this character be taken out of story + examined psychologically? (Sign of true complexity)
- Avoid: Cliché arcs, lack of contradiction, character as author's ventriloquist dummy"
```
