# Chris Voss — Never Split the Difference (2016)

**Core**: Negotiation is NOT splitting differences. FBI hostage negotiator tactics: emotional intelligence > logical arguments.

**Guiding Principle**: Preparation makes 80% of difference. Negotiation itself only 20%.

## Key Tactics

### 1. BE A MIRROR (Mirroring)

- Repeat last 3 words of what they said (question intonation)
- Example: They: "We need to close by Friday." You: "Close by Friday?"
- **Why**: Makes them explain themselves deeper. Feels like connection (not confrontation)
- **Effect**: Gets concessions without asking

### 2. TACTICAL EMPATHY (Not Sympathy)

- Don't "feel their pain" (weak). Label it (powerful)
- "It seems like X is frustrating you..."
- "It sounds like budget is the real issue..."
- Identify what's REALLY blocking them (often hidden)
- **Effect**: Build trust. They feel understood

### 3. MASTER "NO"

- Most people fear "no". Voss flips it
- "Is there any reason you can't do X?" → Safe to say no
- "No" is WHERE negotiation starts (not ends)
- After no: "So what would work?" → Opens dialogue
- **Why**: "Yes" is often reflexive lie. "No" = thinking

### 4. TRIGGER "THAT'S RIGHT"

- Get them to say "that's right" (not "yes")
- Summarize their position from their view
- "So what you're saying is X, Y, Z..."
- When they say "that's right" = real agreement
- **Effect**: Breakthrough moment. They're bought in.

### 5. CALIBRATED QUESTIONS (Not Ultimatums)

- Don't make demands. Ask questions
- "How am I supposed to do that?"
- "What would you need from me to make that work?"
- "How does that affect the timeline?"
- **Why**: Makes them solve problem WITH you (not against you)

### 6. FIND THE BLACK SWAN

- Unknown unknowns. Information counterparty doesn't know you have
- Hidden assumptions/constraints they haven't mentioned
- Example: Budget constraint isn't real constraint. Personal reputation is.
- Dig for what's REALLY stopping deal
- **Effect**: Unblocks everything when found

## Framework

1. **Active Listening** — Shut up. Listen 70%. Talk 30%
2. **Tactical Empathy** — Label emotions. Show you understand them
3. **Mirroring** — Repeat to deepen (not mock)
4. **Calibrated Questions** — Ask "how/what" not "yes/no"
5. **Find Black Swan** — What aren't they saying?
6. **Reach "That's Right"** — Real agreement, not hollow "yes"

## Real Negotiation Trajectory

- Start: Both sides posturing
- Middle: Emotional venting (let it happen)
- Later: Real issues surface (BLACK SWANS)
- Breakthrough: "That's right" moment
- Resolution: Actual deal based on truth

## Tactical Empathy in Action

**Hostage case**: Instead of "Release the hostage or die," Voss asked:

- "Help me understand what you need..." (invitation, not demand)
- Mirrored: "So you're concerned about..."
- Labeled: "It sounds like respect/safety matters most..."
- Calibrated Q: "What would make this work for you?"

**Result**: Hostages released, suspect survived, changed FBI's approach forever.

## Common Mistakes

- ❌ Aggressive demands (triggers resistance)
- ❌ Logical arguments alone (emotion > logic)
- ❌ Splitting differences (leaves both sides unhappy)
- ❌ "Yes" chasing (often false)
- ❌ Ignoring emotions (they're the real deal)
