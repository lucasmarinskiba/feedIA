# Offer Architecture (Cross-Book Framework)

**Core**: Offer = intersection of market positioning + customer psychology + influence stacking + price perception + behavioral triggers.

## Components

### 1. POSITIONING CONTEXT (Dunford — Obviously Awesome)

- 5 components: Competitive alternatives + Unique attributes + Value themes + Target market + Market category.
- **Why first**: Frame sets mental ladder. Customer can't evaluate offer outside context.
- Example: "We help [customer] achieve [benefit]" vs [alternative].

### 2. CUSTOMER AS HERO (Miller — StoryBrand)

- Customer = protagonist. Offer = guide tool (not hero itself).
- SB7: Character (customer) → Problem (3 levels: external/internal/philosophical) → Guide (empathy + authority) → Plan → CTA → Avoid failure → Success.
- **Why essential**: Emotional resonance. Customer buys transformation, not features.

### 3. 6 INFLUENCE WEAPONS STACK (Cialdini — Influence)

- Reciprocity (give first lead magnet)
- Commitment (small asks → escalate)
- Social proof (testimonials from similar people)
- Authority (specific credentials, third-party)
- Liking (similarity, compliments, shared goals)
- Scarcity (real/perceived, exclusivity, loss frame)
- **Stacking rule**: Combine 3-4 weapons. Combined > single.

### 4. PRICING PSYCHOLOGY (Poundstone — Priceless)

- Coherent arbitrariness: Price constructed in mind, not found in market.
- **Anchor high first** (everything downstream = relative to anchor).
- Weber's Law: Perceive % change, not $ amount. 4x price ≠ 4x value perceived.
- 99-cent illusion: $9.99 ≠ $10 (bargain signal).
- Prestige pricing: Higher price = perceived quality.
- **Application**: Show premium offer first. Everything else = bargain.

### 5. BEHAVIORAL IRRATIONALITY (Ariely — Predictably Irrational)

- Relativity: All decisions relative to anchor.
- Zero-cost effect: FREE triggers loss aversion.
- Social norms vs market norms: Don't price favors (kills relationship).
- Ownership bias: Customers overvalue own choices. Use in retention.
- Expectations literal: Expensive wine tastes better (same wine, different price). Price = quality signal.
- **Application**: Use expectations to anchor perception.

## Example Build

**Product**: Productivity app

1. **Positioning** (Dunford):
   - Alternatives: Notion, Asana, paper
   - Unique: AI-powered batching + auto-prioritization
   - Value theme: "Time freedom via intelligent automation"
   - Target: Overwhelmed solopreneurs
   - Category: "Productivity automation" (new market niche)

2. **Hero Narrative** (Miller):
   - Customer: Freelancer drowning in admin tasks
   - Problem: External (admin), Internal (overwhelm), Philosophical (time = freedom)
   - Guide (app) + Plan (3-click setup) + CTA (7-day free trial) + Success (10 hours/week back)

3. **Influence Stack** (Cialdini):
   - Reciprocity: Free template pack (lead magnet)
   - Social proof: "Used by 50K+ freelancers" (specific number)
   - Authority: "Built by ex-Slack PM" (credentials)
   - Scarcity: "Early bird price $29/mo (regular $49)"

4. **Pricing** (Poundstone):
   - Anchor high first: Show $99/mo "Pro" tier
   - Then reveal $49/mo "Standard" (seems cheap)
   - Then $29/mo early bird (exclusive, limited)
   - Price signals quality (not cheap = trustworthy)

5. **Behavioral** (Ariely):
   - Expectations: "AI-powered" (word alone = 3x perceived value)
   - Relativity: Everything relative to $99 anchor
   - Zero-cost: "Free trial" (loss aversion triggered = trial conversion high)
   - Ownership: "Your workflow" (customize = ownership bias lock-in)
