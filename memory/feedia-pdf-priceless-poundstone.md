# William Poundstone — Priceless (2010)

**Core**: Prices constructed in minds, not found in markets. Coherent arbitrariness = valuations relative/stable, amounts absolute/arbitrary (context-dependent).

**Psychophysics Laws**:

- **Weber's Law** — Perceive changes RELATIVE (%) not absolute ($)
  - 10lb added to 40lb = very noticeable; to 150lb = imperceptible
  - 18oz → 16.3oz shrink invisible if package doesn't change

- **Stevens Power Law** — 4x stimulus = 2x subjective perception
  - $10 bonus ≠ half-valuation of $20 (subjectively ~40% more)
  - Luz: 60W + 60W ≠ twice as bright

- **Adaptation Level** (Helson) — Senses adapt to baseline, register changes
  - Chocolate temp before/after hot water = radical perception difference
  - Price anchor = baseline for all future prices

**Anchoring & Adjustment**:

- Random number (10 vs 65) → 2x difference in UN nations estimate (25% vs 45%)
- Mecanismo: Anchor → insufficient mental adjustment → bias persists

**Pricing Applications**:

- High prices as anchor (Broadway $480 → everything else = bargain)
- Invisible increases (shrink quantity not price, redesign packaging)

**Loss Aversion** (Kahneman & Tversky):

- Perder $X = 2x más painful than ganar $X
- Frame: "No pierdes" > "Ganas"

**Illusions**:

- 99¢ effect — $9.99 NOT perceptually close to $10 (penny signals bargain)
- Decoy pricing — tercera opción peor → mid-tier becomes "obvious" choice
- Prestige pricing — Cartier Tank $3K vs Rolex $30K = status difference (8.7x price, 1.5x "better")

**Anti-patterns**: Rational logic ('should' cost X), no high anchor first, low price early, matching competitors, no 99-cent psychology, forget loss-aversion framing.
